02
Three engineering fixes required for certification
Estimated total · $100K · 10–14 weeks
01

Replace WireGuard + HPKE for CSfC / CNSA 2.0.

WireGuard is not CNSA-approved, not on the CSfC Components List, and lacks IKEv2. HPKE has no FIPS 140-3 validation. C1 / C2 share a single ECDH root, which violates CSfC independence. CNSA 2.0 networking deadline is 01 Jan 2027. Replace with NIAP-evaluated IPsec / IKEv2 plus ML-KEM-1024 hybrid post-quantum exchange. [CNSSP-15 Annex B · CSfC VPN CP §3.2]

Timeline 8–12 wks
02

Fix AES-GCM nonce management.

C1 derives key and IV from HKDF deterministically. Nonce reuse causes catastrophic plaintext leak plus GHASH key recovery. Switch to an explicit per-packet counter following TLS 1.3 convention. [NIST SP 800-38D §8.3 · RFC 8446 §5.3]

Timeline 1–2 wks
03

Bind Passport JSON to C2 via AAD.

Passport metadata sits between C2 and C3, protected by C3 alone. A C3 compromise exposes classification markings and routing. Include an HMAC as AAD inside C2 AES-256-GCM. [NIST SP 800-38D §5.2.1.1]

Timeline 1–2 wks
Moat. These fixes are themselves the IP. A CSfC-certified, CNSA 2.0, post-quantum-ready crypto stack is a competitive position. Most competitors will not ship hybrid PQ key exchange before 2028.
